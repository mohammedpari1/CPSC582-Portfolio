01IDENTITY · INTERACTION · VISUAL DESIGN

Family of Buttons

An interactive family of personal buttons that reveals who we are—and what connects us.

My individual contribution was a Navigator identity, inspired by travel and exploring new places. I developed an airplane-themed personal page with a blue-and-white visual language, travel highlights, and connections to teammates.

The design challenge

Represent personality through a simple button while making similarities and differences between team members easy to discover.

My design decisions

I chose a flight motif to communicate exploration, soft rounded cards for an approachable tone, and personal travel details to make the page feel distinct rather than generic.

What I learnedPersonal expression works best when visual choices and interaction goals reinforce each other.

02VOICE INPUT · PROTOTYPING · LEARNING

Teaching Blender
through Voice

Exploring how a human expert can guide a beginner through a complex 3D tool using spoken commands.

Our team compared several approaches, including shared mouse control and an AI hover assistant, before selecting voice commands as the main interaction.

Iteration mattered

The first mode, “Blender does it,” executes recognized commands. We then identified a learning problem: watching automation is not the same as practicing. The second mode, “Learner does it,” displays instructions and shortcuts for the learner to perform.

Testing and limitations

Team testing exposed microphone selection, rapid speech, and misheard command problems. The prototype added a typed-command fallback. It remained English-only, used a fixed command set, and did not implement a true two-computer remote connection.

What I learnedGood interaction design should support the user's deeper goal—not just complete a task quickly.

03 / MY DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
“An interaction should not only work. It should make sense to the person using it.”
01 / Start with people

Understand what someone is trying to express, accomplish, or learn.

02 / Explore before choosing

Sketch different directions and compare tradeoffs before settling on a design.

03 / Improve through feedback

Treat limitations and unexpected behavior as opportunities to iterate.

04 / REFLECTION

From expressing identity
to supporting learning.

My design journey is not just about making more polished interfaces. It is about asking better questions.

EARLIER

What should this feel like?

In Family of Buttons, I focused on turning my interests and personality into a recognizable interaction and visual theme.

LATER

What should this help someone do?

In the Blender project, our team examined how the interaction affected learning. The second mode emerged because simply performing the action did not fully serve the learner.

NEXT

What would I test further?

I would test prototypes with people outside the design team, observe where they struggle, and use that evidence to guide the next iteration.
